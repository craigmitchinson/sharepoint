Text web part · lead paragraph

Ideas come from the people closest to the work. Tell us the problem in your own words; you do not need to know which technology might solve it.

Text web part

What makes a good Frontier idea

  • A real problem a customer or colleague has today
  • You can say what better looks like: a better service, less time, less risk
  • You can say who it affects and roughly how often
  • Something small enough to test in six weeks
  • Any technology, a new way of working, or both: we choose what fits the problem
Text web part and Button web part

What happens next

  1. The Frontier panel reviews new ideas every fortnight.
  2. You hear back within 10 working days: chosen, shortlisted or parked, with the reason.
  3. If it is chosen, you join the experiment as the problem owner.

Do I need to be technical?
No. You bring the problem and what good would look like. Frontier brings the technology and the people to build and test it.
Will it take me away from my day job?
Problem owners usually give two to four hours a week for the six weeks of a test, agreed with your line manager before it starts.
What if my idea is not chosen?
You get the reason. Parked ideas stay visible and are looked at again each quarter, and many go straight to a Lab through the CCS front door instead.
Can we use real customer data?
Not by default. Experiments use synthetic or masked data in a sandbox. Real data needs Data Protection sign-off before the test starts.
